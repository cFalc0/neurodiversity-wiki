## Key Researchers

- **Russell A. Barkley** — Primary proponent of emotional dysregulation as a core ADHD feature; coined Emotional Impulsiveness (EI) and Deficient Emotional Self-Regulation (DESR)
- **Amy F.T. Arnsten** (Yale) — PFC catecholamine circuit research; inverted-U model of NE/DA in executive function and emotional regulation
- **Philip Shaw** (NIH/George Washington) — Cortical maturation delay studies; three-framework model for ADHD + ED
- **Predrag Petrovic & F. Xavier Castellanos** — Hot/cool PFC model; top-down dysregulation in ADHD
- **Leslie Hulvershorn** (Indiana University) — Amygdala-rACC functional connectivity in emotional lability

## Notes

- "Emotional dysregulation" in ADHD literature appears under multiple terms: Deficient Emotional Self-Regulation (DESR), Emotional Impulsiveness (EI), emotional lability, affective dysregulation, mood dysregulation
- DSM-5-TR does not list ED as a formal ADHD criterion — this is an active area of debate
- Prevalence estimates vary widely (30–70% in adults) depending on measure and definition used
- The ADHD–BPD distinction is critical and frequently misunderstood clinically; ED feature overlap is high but mechanisms and clinical trajectory differ

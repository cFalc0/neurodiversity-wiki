# Raw Sources — Dyslexia

## Key Organizations

- https://dyslexiaida.org/ — International Dyslexia Association. Best single resource for definitions, research briefs, and fact sheets. IDA's definition of dyslexia is the most widely cited in clinical and educational settings.
- https://dyslexia.yale.edu/ — Yale Center for Dyslexia & Creativity. Home of Shaywitz et al. research. Good explainers on the neuroscience of reading and dyslexic strengths.
- https://www.bdadyslexia.org.uk/ — British Dyslexia Association. UK-focused but excellent practical and workplace resources.
- https://www.understood.org/en/articles/understanding-dyslexia — Understood.org overview. Accessible, non-clinical, good for sharing with family/employers.
- https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dyslexia/ — NHS page on dyslexia. Clean clinical overview.

## Key Researchers to Follow

- **Sally Shaywitz & Bennett Shaywitz** (Yale) — Neuroimaging studies showing how dyslexic brains process reading differently. Co-founded the Yale Center for Dyslexia & Creativity.
- **Maryanne Wolf** (UCLA) — Author of *Proust and the Squid* and *Reader Come Home*. Work on reading neuroscience and the "reading brain." Pioneer on naming speed (RAN) and dyslexia.
- **Margaret Snowling** (Oxford) — Phonological deficit hypothesis, longitudinal studies on dyslexia in children.
- **Stanislas Dehaene** (Collège de France) — *Reading in the Brain*. Neuroimaging of the visual word form area and reading circuitry.

## Notes

- "Dyslexia" and "reading disorder" are often used interchangeably in research literature; DSM-5 classifies it under "Specific Learning Disorder with impairment in reading"
- Comorbidity with ADHD is high — estimated 25–40% of people with dyslexia also have ADHD
- Dyslexia affects ~10–15% of the population; some estimates go higher depending on diagnostic threshold
- The term "word blindness" predates "dyslexia" — used by W. Pringle Morgan in 1896 in the British Medical Journal
